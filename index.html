8.1

Introduction

Mark four points and join them in order. If all four lie on one line you get a line segment. If three lie on one line you get a triangle. If no three are collinear you get a closed figure with four sides, called a quadrilateral. This chapter deals with the convex kind, where the figure does not fold inward.

4 vertices

The corner points A, B, C and D.

4 sides

AB, BC, CD and DA.

4 angles

∠A, ∠B, ∠C and ∠D, formed at the vertices.

2 diagonals

AC and BD, joining opposite vertices.

Look around the room: the blackboard, a book cover, a table top. Most are rectangles, and a rectangle is a parallelogram, so everything you prove about parallelograms holds for them too.

8.2

Angle sum property of a quadrilateral

Angle sum property

The sum of the angles of a quadrilateral is 360°.

GivenQuadrilateral ABCD with diagonal AC.
To prove∠A + ∠B + ∠C + ∠D = 360°
StatementReason
  1. In ΔADC: ∠DAC + ∠ACD + ∠D = 180° … (1)Angle sum property of a triangle
  2. In ΔABC: ∠CAB + ∠ACB + ∠B = 180° … (2)Angle sum property of a triangle
  3. Adding (1) and (2): ∠DAC + ∠CAB + ∠ACD + ∠ACB + ∠B + ∠D = 360°Adding equal sums
  4. ∠DAC + ∠CAB = ∠A and ∠ACD + ∠ACB = ∠CDiagonal AC lies inside ∠A and ∠C
  5. ∴ ∠A + ∠B + ∠C + ∠D = 360°Substituting in step 3
8.3

Types of quadrilaterals

Pick a type, then use the sliders. The shape keeps its defining property while everything else is free, and the readout names what you have built.

Parallelogram

Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

Rectangle

A parallelogram with one right angle, so all four angles are 90°.

Rhombus

A parallelogram with all four sides equal.

Square

A rectangle with all four sides equal. It is also a rhombus.

Kite

Two distinct pairs of equal adjacent sides.

Trapezium

Exactly one pair of opposite sides parallel. Those are the bases, the others are the legs.

  • A square is a rectangle and also a rhombus.
  • A rectangle or a rhombus is not necessarily a square.
  • A kite is not a parallelogram, and a trapezium is not a parallelogram, because a parallelogram needs both pairs of opposite sides parallel.

Definition note. Some books, including the NCERT text, call a parallelogram a trapezium too, since it has at least one pair of parallel sides. Here a trapezium means exactly one pair, which keeps the six types separate in the table below.

* A kite has two pairs of equal adjacent sides, not four equal sides. ** A rhombus has equal diagonals only when it is a square.

Opposite sides parallelBoth pairsBoth pairsBoth pairsBoth pairsNoOne pair
All four sides equalNoNoYesYesNo*No
All angles 90°NoYesYesNoNoNo

8.4

Properties of a parallelogram

Seven results, in pairs: each theorem is followed by its converse. Every figure below is a parallelogram ABCD with A, B and D draggable. C follows automatically, because that is exactly what a parallelogram is.

Theorem 8.1

A diagonal of a parallelogram divides it into two congruent triangles.

GivenParallelogram ABCD with diagonal AC.
To proveΔABC ≅ ΔCDA
StatementReason
  1. BC ∥ AD and AC is a transversal, so ∠BCA = ∠DACAlternate interior angles
  2. AB ∥ DC and AC is a transversal, so ∠BAC = ∠DCAAlternate interior angles
  3. AC = CACommon side
  4. ∴ ΔABC ≅ ΔCDAASA congruence rule
Theorem 8.2

In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal.

GivenParallelogram ABCD.
To proveAB = DC and AD = BC
StatementReason
  1. ΔABC ≅ ΔCDATheorem 8.1
  2. ∴ AB = DC and BC = DACorresponding parts of congruent triangles (CPCT)
Theorem 8.3 · converse of 8.2

If each pair of opposite sides of a quadrilateral is equal, then it is a parallelogram.

GivenQuadrilateral ABCD with AB = CD and AD = BC. Draw diagonal AC.
To proveABCD is a parallelogram
StatementReason
  1. In ΔABC and ΔCDA: AB = CDGiven
  2. BC = DAGiven
  3. AC = CACommon side
  4. ΔABC ≅ ΔCDASSS congruence rule
  5. ∠BAC = ∠DCACPCT
  6. AB ∥ DCAlternate interior angles are equal (transversal AC)
  7. ∠BCA = ∠DAC, so BC ∥ ADCPCT, then alternate angles equal
  8. ∴ ABCD is a parallelogramBoth pairs of opposite sides are parallel

Try it yourself in the converse tester at the end of section 8.5.

Theorem 8.4

In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal.

GivenParallelogram ABCD.
To prove∠A = ∠C and ∠B = ∠D
StatementReason
  1. AD ∥ BC and AB is a transversal, so ∠A + ∠B = 180°Interior angles on the same side of a transversal
  2. AB ∥ DC and BC is a transversal, so ∠B + ∠C = 180°Interior angles on the same side of a transversal
  3. ∠A + ∠B = ∠B + ∠C, so ∠A = ∠CFrom steps 1 and 2
  4. AB ∥ DC and AD is a transversal, so ∠A + ∠D = 180°Interior angles on the same side of a transversal
  5. ∠A + ∠B = ∠A + ∠D, so ∴ ∠B = ∠DFrom steps 1 and 4
Theorem 8.5 · converse of 8.4

If each pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral is equal, then it is a parallelogram.

GivenQuadrilateral ABCD with ∠A = ∠C and ∠B = ∠D.
To proveABCD is a parallelogram
StatementReason
  1. ∠A + ∠B + ∠C + ∠D = 360°Angle sum of a quadrilateral
  2. 2∠A + 2∠B = 360°Replace ∠C by ∠A and ∠D by ∠B
  3. ∠A + ∠B = 180°Divide by 2
  4. AD ∥ BCInterior angles on the same side of transversal AB add up to 180°
  5. ∠B + ∠C = ∠B + ∠A = 180°, so AB ∥ DCSame argument with transversal BC
  6. ∴ ABCD is a parallelogramBoth pairs of opposite sides are parallel
Theorem 8.6

The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.

GivenParallelogram ABCD whose diagonals AC and BD meet at O.
To proveOA = OC and OB = OD
StatementReason
  1. In ΔAOB and ΔCOD: ∠OAB = ∠OCDAB ∥ DC, transversal AC, alternate angles
  2. ∠OBA = ∠ODCAB ∥ DC, transversal BD, alternate angles
  3. AB = CDOpposite sides of a parallelogram (Theorem 8.2)
  4. ΔAOB ≅ ΔCODASA congruence rule
  5. ∴ OA = OC and OB = ODCPCT
Theorem 8.7 · converse of 8.6

If the diagonals of a quadrilateral bisect each other, then it is a parallelogram.

GivenQuadrilateral ABCD with diagonals meeting at O, OA = OC and OB = OD.
To proveABCD is a parallelogram
StatementReason
  1. In ΔAOB and ΔCOD: OA = OC and OB = ODGiven
  2. ∠AOB = ∠CODVertically opposite angles
  3. ΔAOB ≅ ΔCODSAS congruence rule
  4. ∠ABO = ∠CDOCPCT
  5. AB ∥ CDAlternate interior angles are equal (transversal BD)
  6. Similarly ΔAOD ≅ ΔCOB, so ∠OAD = ∠OCB and BC ∥ ADSAS, CPCT, alternate angles (transversal AC)
  7. ∴ ABCD is a parallelogramBoth pairs of opposite sides are parallel
Example 1

Show that each angle of a rectangle is a right angle.

A rectangle is a parallelogram in which one angle is a right angle. Let ABCD be a rectangle with ∠A = 90°.

StatementReason
  1. AD ∥ BC and AB is a transversal, so ∠A + ∠B = 180°Interior angles on the same side
  2. ∠B = 180° − 90° = 90°Since ∠A = 90°
  3. ∠C = ∠A = 90° and ∠D = ∠B = 90°Opposite angles of a parallelogram (Theorem 8.4)
  4. ∴ Each angle of a rectangle is 90°
Example 2

Show that the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular to each other.

Let ABCD be a rhombus whose diagonals meet at O.

StatementReason
  1. AB = BC = CD = DASides of a rhombus
  2. In ΔAOD and ΔCOD: OA = OCDiagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other
  3. OD = OD and AD = CDCommon side; sides of the rhombus
  4. ΔAOD ≅ ΔCODSSS congruence rule
  5. ∠AOD = ∠CODCPCT
  6. ∠AOD + ∠COD = 180°Linear pair
  7. 2∠AOD = 180°, so ∴ ∠AOD = 90°Substituting step 5
8.4

More worked examples on parallelograms

Example 3

ABC is an isosceles triangle with AB = AC. AD bisects the exterior angle PAC and CD ∥ AB. Show that (i) ∠DAC = ∠BCA and (ii) ABCD is a parallelogram.

StatementReason
  1. ∠ABC = ∠ACBAngles opposite equal sides AB and AC
  2. ∠PAC = ∠ABC + ∠ACB = 2∠ACB … (1)Exterior angle of a triangle
  3. ∠PAC = 2∠DAC … (2)AD bisects ∠PAC
  4. 2∠DAC = 2∠ACB, so ∠DAC = ∠BCA (part i)From (1) and (2)
  5. ∠DAC and ∠BCA are alternate angles for BC and AD with transversal AC, so BC ∥ ADAlternate angles are equal
  6. BA ∥ CDGiven
  7. ∴ ABCD is a parallelogram (part ii)Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel
Example 4

Two parallel lines l and m are cut by a transversal p. Show that the quadrilateral formed by the bisectors of the interior angles is a rectangle.

Let PS ∥ QR and let p cut them at A and C. The bisectors of ∠PAC and ∠ACQ meet at B. The bisectors of ∠ACR and ∠SAC meet at D. We show ABCD is a rectangle.

StatementReason
  1. ∠PAC = ∠ACRAlternate angles, PS ∥ QR
  2. ½∠PAC = ½∠ACR, so ∠BAC = ∠ACDAB and CD are angle bisectors
  3. AB ∥ DCAlternate angles ∠BAC, ∠ACD are equal (transversal AC)
  4. Similarly ∠ACB = ∠CAD, so BC ∥ ADSame argument with ∠ACQ = ∠SAC
  5. ABCD is a parallelogramBoth pairs of opposite sides are parallel
  6. ∠PAC + ∠CAS = 180°Linear pair
  7. ½∠PAC + ½∠CAS = 90°, so ∠BAC + ∠CAD = 90°, that is ∠BAD = 90°Halving step 6
  8. ∴ ABCD is a rectangleA parallelogram with one right angle
Example 5

Show that the bisectors of the angles of a parallelogram form a rectangle.

Let P, Q, R, S be the points where the bisectors of ∠A and ∠B, ∠B and ∠C, ∠C and ∠D, ∠D and ∠A of parallelogram ABCD meet.

StatementReason
  1. In ΔASD: ∠DAS + ∠ADS = ½∠A + ½∠D = ½(∠A + ∠D)DS and AS bisect ∠D and ∠A
  2. ½(∠A + ∠D) = ½ × 180° = 90°∠A and ∠D are interior angles on the same side
  3. ∠DAS + ∠ADS + ∠DSA = 180°, so ∠DSA = 90°Angle sum of a triangle
  4. ∠PSR = ∠DSA = 90°Vertically opposite angles
  5. Similarly ∠APB = ∠SPQ = 90°, ∠PQR = 90° and ∠SRQ = 90°Same argument at B, C
  6. ∴ PQRS is a rectangleEqual opposite angles make it a parallelogram (Theorem 8.5), and it has a right angle
8.5

Another condition for a parallelogram

Theorem 8.8 is the shortest test: one pair of sides that is both equal and parallel is already enough.

Theorem 8.8

A quadrilateral is a parallelogram if a pair of opposite sides is equal and parallel.

GivenQuadrilateral ABCD with AB ∥ CD and AB = CD. Draw diagonal AC.
To proveABCD is a parallelogram
StatementReason
  1. In ΔABC and ΔCDA: AB = CDGiven
  2. ∠BAC = ∠DCAAB ∥ CD, transversal AC, alternate angles
  3. AC = CACommon side
  4. ΔABC ≅ ΔCDASAS congruence rule
  5. ∠BCA = ∠DACCPCT
  6. BC ∥ ADAlternate angles are equal (transversal AC)
  7. ∴ AB ∥ CD and BC ∥ AD, so ABCD is a parallelogramDefinition
Example 6

ABCD is a parallelogram. P and Q are the mid-points of AB and CD. AQ meets DP at S and BQ meets CP at R. Show that (i) APCQ is a parallelogram, (ii) DPBQ is a parallelogram, (iii) PSQR is a parallelogram.

StatementReason
  1. (i) AP ∥ QCAB ∥ CD
  2. AP = ½AB, CQ = ½CD and AB = CD, so AP = QCMid-points; opposite sides of a parallelogram
  3. ∴ APCQ is a parallelogramTheorem 8.8
  4. (ii) DQ ∥ PB and DQ = PB, so DPBQ is a parallelogramSame argument, Theorem 8.8
  5. (iii) SP ∥ QRSP is part of DP, QR is part of QB, and DP ∥ QB by (ii)
  6. SQ ∥ PRSQ is part of AQ, PR is part of PC, and AQ ∥ PC by (i)
  7. ∴ PSQR is a parallelogramBoth pairs of opposite sides are parallel

8.6

The mid-point theorem

Measure the segment joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle. It is parallel to the third side and exactly half as long. Drag the triangle and check.

Theorem 8.9

The line segment joining the mid-points of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side, and is half of it.

GivenΔABC, with E the mid-point of AB and F the mid-point of AC.
To proveEF ∥ BC and EF = ½BC
StatementReason
  1. Produce EF to D so that FD = EF. Join CD.Construction
  2. In ΔAEF and ΔCDF: AF = CFF is the mid-point of AC
  3. ∠AFE = ∠CFDVertically opposite angles
  4. EF = DFConstruction
  5. ΔAEF ≅ ΔCDFSAS congruence rule
  6. AE = CD and ∠AEF = ∠CDFCPCT
  7. AE ∥ DCAlternate angles ∠AEF, ∠CDF are equal (transversal ED)
  8. BE = AE = DC, and BE ∥ DCE is the mid-point of AB; steps 6 and 7
  9. BCDE is a parallelogramA pair of opposite sides is equal and parallel (Theorem 8.8)
  10. ED ∥ BC and ED = BCOpposite sides of a parallelogram
  11. ∴ EF ∥ BC and EF = ½ED = ½BCF is the mid-point of ED
Theorem 8.10 · converse of 8.9

The line drawn through the mid-point of one side of a triangle, parallel to another side, bisects the third side.

GivenΔABC, E the mid-point of AB, and a line l through E with l ∥ BC, meeting AC at F.
To proveAF = CF
StatementReason
  1. Draw CM ∥ BA, meeting l at D.Construction
  2. EB ∥ DC and ED ∥ BCBA ∥ CM, and l ∥ BC (Given)
  3. BCDE is a parallelogramBoth pairs of opposite sides are parallel
  4. DC = EB = AEOpposite sides; E is the mid-point of AB
  5. In ΔAEF and ΔCDF: ∠AEF = ∠CDFAB ∥ CM, transversal ED, alternate angles
  6. ∠AFE = ∠CFDVertically opposite angles
  7. AE = CDStep 4
  8. ΔAEF ≅ ΔCDFAAS congruence rule
  9. ∴ AF = CFCPCT
Example 7

In ΔABC, D, E and F are the mid-points of AB, BC and CA. Show that joining D, E and F divides ΔABC into four congruent triangles.

StatementReason
  1. DE ∥ AC, DF ∥ BC and EF ∥ ABMid-point theorem (Theorem 8.9)
  2. ADEF, BDFE and DFCE are parallelogramsBoth pairs of opposite sides are parallel
  3. DE is a diagonal of BDFE, so ΔBDE ≅ ΔFEDTheorem 8.1
  4. Similarly ΔDAF ≅ ΔFED (diagonal DF of ADEF) and ΔEFC ≅ ΔFED (diagonal EF of DFCE)Theorem 8.1
  5. ∴ All four triangles are congruent
Example 8

l, m and n are three parallel lines cut by transversals p and q. If l, m and n cut off equal intercepts AB and BC on p, show that they cut off equal intercepts DE and EF on q.

StatementReason
  1. Join A to F, meeting m at G.Construction
  2. In ΔACF, B is the mid-point of ACAB = BC (Given)
  3. BG ∥ CFm ∥ n
  4. G is the mid-point of AFTheorem 8.10
  5. In ΔAFD, G is the mid-point of AF and GE ∥ ADStep 4; m ∥ l
  6. E is the mid-point of DFTheorem 8.10
  7. ∴ DE = EFSo l, m and n cut equal intercepts on q too
